## Bio

Born October 5, 1979, in Ramla; resident of Matzkeret Batya; married to attorney Yehuda Ifargan, mother of three daughters. B.A. in political science and international relations (Hebrew University of Jerusalem, 2006), M.A. in public policy (Tel Aviv University, 2010). Career civil servant: held roles in the Prime Minister's Office and Defense Ministry as an intern/junior staffer, then head of the CEO's office and acting deputy director for administration/HR at the Ministry of Economy and Industry, and director of the government's technological-education institute (Mahat). Appointed acting head of the Labor Administration in 2021 by Labor Minister Yitzhak Smoli, then confirmed as its CEO in 2022 following a search-committee recommendation; served as **Director-General of the Ministry of Labor, 2021–2023**, during which she pushed reforms to Israeli labor law including hybrid-work models and flexible monthly-hours calculation, and worked on integrating Haredi and Arab populations into the workforce. Named to TheMarker's "100 most influential" list and Globes' "50 most influential women" list. After leaving government, became CEO of a philanthropic-fund partnership for development and growth in Israel's north and south, established after October 7. Served in the IDF as a trainee-company commander. Joined Gadi Eisenkot's "ישר" party, announced 2026-06-10, and was placed 8th on the final list.
